# Watcher 364 — source matrix

This is the audit trail behind the interactive exhibit. It records what each witness actually preserves, the exact arithmetic those numbers produce, and where the witnesses or reconstructions collide.

## Reading rule

- **Preserved**: the surviving composition or manuscript gives the number or statement.
- **Pattern indicated**: multiple details align in a repeatable structure; the structure is named without turning it into a verdict.
- **Clear inference**: the conclusion follows directly from what the witness states, but the witness does not state the conclusion in those words.
- **Mathematically certain**: the equation follows exactly from preserved numbers. It establishes the arithmetic and nothing beyond the arithmetic.
- **Computed**: exact arithmetic performed on preserved numbers.
- **Reconstructed**: missing material, fragment order, or a full system restored by a named editor.
- **Collision**: two readings or witnesses disagree. Both stay visible.
- **Absence**: a rule is not present in the surviving text. This is a fact about the evidence, not a dismissal of the system.

## Working synthesis

Enochian time is a recursive covenantal order. It joins the seven-day Sabbath, four 91-day seasons, the 364-day year, the moon’s phases and deficits, fixed festivals, twenty-four priestly courses, seven-year Releases, 49-year Jubilees, and long historical or judgment periods.

The recurring arithmetic is:

| Scale | Exact relation |
|---|---:|
| Quarter | 30 + 30 + 31 = 91 days = 13 weeks |
| Year | 4 × 91 = 364 days = 52 weeks |
| Three-year return | 3 × 364 = 1,092 days = 156 weeks |
| Lunar comparison | 3 × 354 + 30 = 1,092 |
| Priestly return | 6 × 52 = 312 weeks = 13 × 24 courses |
| Jubilee | 7 × 7 = 49 years |
| Grand return | LCM(3, 6, 49) = 294 years |
| Grand return expanded | 6 Jubilees = 49 priestly cycles = 98 triennial starts = 42 Sabbatical cycles |
| Grand course count | 294 × 52 ÷ 24 = 637 complete course rotations |

## 1 Enoch

The numerical readings below were checked against the [Nickelsburg–VanderKam critical translation and apparatus](https://currah.download/pages/bible/reversinghermon/documents/1Enoch_New-Translation_Nickelsburg-1984.pdf), whose [Fortress edition statement](https://ms.fortresspress.com/downloads/9780800699109Preface.pdf) identifies VanderKam as translator of chapters 72–82 and states that the revised translation uses all ancient textual sources. The St. Mary’s selections begin at chapter 83, and the current OCP file does not encode a complete 72–82; neither is used as a control for this section.

### Qumran Jubilees witnesses

1Q17–18; 2Q19–20; 3Q5; 4Q176a/b; 4Q216–224; 11Q12, with some assignments disputed. 4Q216 vi preserves the luminaries governing Sabbaths, festivals, years, year-weeks, Jubilees, and year-cycles. No surviving Hebrew fragment preserves all of Jubilees 6’s 364-day polemic.

### Technical calendars without mishmarot

Technical calendar survey group:

- 4Q324b
- 4Q324d
- 4Q324e
- 4Q326
- 4Q337
- 4Q327 / 4Q394 fragments 1–2
- 4Q394 fragments 3–7 I 1–3
- [6Q17](https://lexicon.qumran-digital.org/transcriptions/6Q17/2023-08-21/index.html)

These measure one 364-day year without priestly-course names. 4Q326 preserves in ink a fourth-day year-head, a Sabbath on the 11th, Unleavened Bread on a fourth day and the Sheaf on the 26th after a Sabbath; the Sabbaths of the 4th, 18th and 25th are restored in its gaps (Qumran-Digital 2023-08-21). Earlier wording here: Sabbaths on days 4/11/18/25, Unleavened Bread on Wednesday day 15, and the Sheaf on Sunday day 26. 6Q17 preserves only “the second month, in it are 30 days.”

The [new 4Q324d reconstruction](https://megillot.haifa.ac.il/images/pdf/Ratzon_and_Ben-Dov_4Q324d.compressed.pdf) joins forty-two fragments formerly distributed among several sigla. It restores 30/30/31 quarters, Wednesday season heads, fixed festivals, and added 31st days. The joins and missing dates remain reconstruction.

## Long-number and cross-cultural control corpus

These witnesses answer a separate question: where do 6,000, 70/72, 432,000, 25,920, and 25,920,000 actually occur? Numerical resemblance is logged; literary dependence is not presumed.

Two products therefore remain visible without being backdated:

- `6,000 × 72 = 432,000` is exact. The inspected ancient witnesses keep the factors in separate textual families and do not state the multiplication.
- `360 × 72 = 25,920` is exact. Riccioli states that model in 1651; the inspected Enochic, Jubilees, Qumran, Hipparchan, and Ptolemaic witnesses do not.

## The texts’ own checks

- 1 Enoch 89–90: 12 → 35 → 58 → 70 shepherds, each running total stated.
- 11Q5 XXVII: 364 + 52 + 30 = 446 and 3,600 + 446 + 4 = 4,050, both stated.
- 1 Enoch 74:13–16: 2,912 − 2,832 = 80, stated.

## Open questions on the leather

1. Six Jubilees: 294 in Jubilees’ 49-year system versus the Genesis 300-year Enoch span.
2. 4Q208–209: 364-day triennial reconstruction versus a 354/360 astronomical model.
3. 4Q317: schematic table versus successful observation.
4. 4Q318: 360 versus 364.
5. 4Q319: the Otot table is preserved; the signs’ function is disputed.
6. 4Q320: manuscript order and the unified 294-year compendium are reconstructed.
7. 4Q321: X and dwq do not have one universally demonstrated key.
8. 4Q327 versus 4Q394: separate manuscript or opening of MMT.
9. 4Q324d–i: multiple sigla versus a single rejoined scroll.
10. 4Q322–333: one annal versus distinct course-dated scraps.
11. Temple Scroll: festival convergence without an explicit 364.
12. Day boundary: sunset evidence, morning-first sequences, and possible dual reckoning.
13. No surviving 364-day source gives an explicit seasonal correction rule.
14. Berossus’s 432,000 versus the Sumerian King List’s 241,200 antediluvian total.
15. Grímnismál’s decimal-hundred product 432,000 versus long-hundred product 614,400.
16. Ptolemy’s precessional rate yields 36,000; Riccioli’s chosen rate yields 25,920.
17. The Indic 25,920,000 is six mahayugas, not a 25,920-year precessional circuit.

## The reckoning, tested (added 2026-09-27)

- **Six Jubilees = 293 solar years.** 294 × 364 = 107,016 days; 293 × 365.2422 = 107,015.96 days; difference 0.04 day. **Computed.** 4Q319 states the 294-year cycle; 1 Enoch 72/74 state 364 and 1,092; no inspected text states the seasonal return.
- **Inside the cycle.** 49 × 364 = 17,836 days against 17,896.9 solar days: 61 days behind per Jubilee, one year behind after six. 1,092 days = 37 synodic months − 0.63 day. **Computed.**
- **Nisan 14, 30 CE.** Conjunction Wed 22 March, c. 20:10 Jerusalem mean time (Meeus ch. 49; ΔT c. 2.8 h). First crescent Fri 24 March evening; Nisan 14 = Friday 7 April 30, JDN 1,732,112. Full moon Thu 6 April c. 22:00. Agrees with Humphreys & Waddington, *Nature* 306 (1983) 743–746.
- **Nisan 14, 33 CE.** Conjunction Thu 19 March, c. 13:00. First crescent Fri 20 March evening; Nisan 14 = Friday 3 April 33, JDN 1,733,204. Full moon that evening c. 17:15; partial lunar eclipse at moonrise per Humphreys & Waddington; visibility disputed by Schaefer, *QJRAS* 31 (1990) 53–67.
- **Weight of the 1,092.** Both dates are Fridays from the Gospel record. Any two Fridays three Julian years apart in one season are 1,092 or 1,099 days apart; these are 1,092 = 3 × 364. The count adds that both fall on the same day of the same written month. It does not decide between them.
- **The written year's Passover.** Year-head Wednesday (4Q320, 4Q326); 1/14 is always Tuesday, 1/15 Wednesday. Jaubert, *La date de la Cène* (1957), reads the Synoptic meal and John's preparation-day crucifixion together through it. **Her reading; the texts state the two Passovers.**

## The caseback (added 2026-09-28)

The reverse of the watch shows one thing: which of the six portals the sun rises through on the set day, with the day and night parts of that month. **Text states**, 1 Enoch 72:2–32: six openings in the east and six in the west; the sun rises through one and sets through its match; the year opens in the fourth (day 10 parts, night 8); the sun climbs to the sixth (12/6), turns, descends to the first (6/12), and returns; thirty mornings in each portal, thirty-one at the four turns. The identification of the sixth and first portals with the solstices and the fourth with the equinoxes is **ours**, stated as such on the watch. The model's caseback wheels for 25,920 and 432,000 years and its seventy-tick engraving are not carried over (**absent**).
